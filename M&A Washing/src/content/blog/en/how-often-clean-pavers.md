Concrete pavers are tough, but they are not waterproof. The concrete absorbs water, soil and organic residue: over time the surface darkens and moss and grass settle into the joints.

## Signs not to ignore

- **Green joints or joints full of grass.** Roots widen the joints and the pavers start to move.
- **A slippery surface when wet.** This is a film of algae, especially dangerous on ramps and sloping paths.
- **Some areas darker than others.** Shaded areas and spots near gutters get dirty first.
- **Stains the rain will not remove.** Oil, rotting leaves and rust soak into the pores of the concrete.

## How often to act

Around Padua, with damp winters and hot summers, a full wash **every one or two years** keeps paving in good condition. Heavily shaded surfaces or those near trees may need it every year; those in full sun even less often.

Between washes, a little goes a long way: sweep leaves before they rot and treat oil stains straight away with an absorbent.

## Why a household pressure washer is not enough

A household pressure washer used with a pencil jet leaves light and dark stripes and can wash the sand out of the joints. The result is a patchy surface and less stable pavers.

An even result needs a **surface cleaner**, a rotating disc that keeps the nozzle at a constant distance, and the right pressure for the paver type. After washing, the joints should be checked and re-sanded if needed.

## Making the result last

A water-repellent **protective treatment**, applied to the dry surface after washing, reduces how much water and dirt the pavers absorb and slows moss regrowth. It is optional, but on exposed surfaces it makes a real difference.
