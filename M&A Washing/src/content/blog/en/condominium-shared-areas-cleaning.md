Courtyards, garage ramps, entrance paths and boundary walls are a condominium's calling card. Washing them takes some planning, because the areas are large and used every day.

## 1. Site visit and quote

The first step is a site visit with the building manager or a board member. We measure the surfaces, identify problem spots (shaded areas, oil stains in parking bays, damaged joints) and check for a water tap and power socket.

A good quote is **itemised**: which areas, which work, any optional treatments. That makes it easier to discuss at the owners' meeting and compare with other offers.

## 2. Plan the work by zones

In condominiums it pays to split the job into zones. Part of the courtyard stays usable at all times and cars only move when needed.

## 3. Notify residents

A few days before, post a notice with dates, times and the zones involved, asking residents to clear parking bays and not to hang laundry above the work areas.

## 4. After washing

When the job is done we check surfaces, drains and joints together. If agreed, the protective treatment is applied once the surface is dry.

With a wash every one or two years, shared areas stay tidy and costly paving repairs are avoided.
